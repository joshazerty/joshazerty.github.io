Research directions

Four directions, one underlying complaint: the system that gets computed is usually not the system that gets measured. Each of these closes part of that gap.

Catalysis under operating conditions

A supported catalyst under working conditions is not one structure. It restructures, changes oxidation state, picks up hydroxyls and water, and sometimes dissolves into the medium that was meant to be the reactant. I work out which species are actually populated at a given temperature, pressure and solvent activity, and only then ask what they do. Solvation belongs inside that question, not bolted on afterwards as a correction.

  • ab initio thermodynamics
  • global optimisation
  • metadynamics
  • QM/MM FEP

Automated reaction mechanism discovery

The usual failure in a mechanistic study is not a bad barrier, it is a step nobody thought to draw. Automated exploration removes that assumption: enumerate candidate elementary steps, locate and verify the transition states, then let the kinetics decide which route carries the flux. I work on making that pipeline reliable enough to trust unattended, and on using learned models to steer the search instead of enumerating blindly.

  • reaction network exploration
  • automated TS search
  • IRC verification
  • graph analysis

Machine-learned potentials for transition metals

Sampling at DFT cost is what limits everything above, and foundation interatomic potentials are nearly good enough to lift that limit. Nearly. Open-shell transition-metal chemistry is where they are weakest, and spin state is usually not represented at all, so a model that handles organic reactions cleanly can miss the surface crossing that sets the rate. I characterise those failures and fine-tune around them, releasing the benchmarks rather than keeping them in-house.

  • MACE
  • UMA
  • fine-tuning
  • spin-state benchmarks

Kinetics that can be tested

A free-energy diagram is not yet a prediction. Turning one into a turnover frequency, an apparent activation energy, a reaction order or a kinetic isotope effect is what makes a mechanism falsifiable, and doing so regularly shows that the tallest barrier is not the step in control. I build micro-kinetic models on computed networks and propagate the uncertainty in the barriers through to the observable, so the comparison with experiment stays honest about what it can settle.

  • micro-kinetic modelling
  • degree of rate control
  • energetic span
  • kinetic isotope effects
