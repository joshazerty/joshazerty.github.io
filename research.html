Research directions

Catalysis under operating conditions

Put a supported catalyst under reaction conditions and it restructures, changes oxidation state, picks up hydroxyls and water, and sometimes dissolves into the medium that was meant to be the reactant. So I start by working out which species are populated at a given temperature, pressure and solvent activity, then ask what those species do. The solvent is in that calculation from the start.

  • ab initio thermodynamics
  • global optimisation
  • metadynamics
  • QM/MM FEP

Automated reaction mechanism discovery

Mechanistic studies fail more often on a step nobody thought to draw than on a badly computed barrier. This bites hardest in homogeneous catalysis, where the resting state, the active species and the off-cycle sinks all compete. Automated exploration enumerates the candidate steps instead, locates and verifies the transition states, and lets the kinetics pick the route that carries the flux.

More of this is now agentic. Give an LLM agent an electronic-structure code, a transition-state search and a kinetic model, and it can propose chemistry, run the calculation that would refute the proposal, and revise on the result. That spends the search budget better than enumeration does. Proposing is the easy half. Most of the effort goes into verification: characterising every structure, confirming every transition state by its imaginary mode and its IRC, so you can check what the agent reports instead of trusting it.

  • agentic AI discovery
  • reaction network exploration
  • automated TS search
  • homogeneous catalysis
  • IRC verification

Spin, chirality and learned potentials

Transition-metal reactions often change spin on the way through, and the rate then depends on where the two surfaces cross rather than on any barrier you can optimise to. Chirality enters the same physics from the other side, through spin selectivity in chiral molecules and the interfaces they form.

This is also where machine-learned potentials break down. Foundation models come close to lifting the cost limit on everything above, but most carry no notion of spin state, so a model that handles organic reactions well can walk straight past the crossing that sets the rate. I measure where they fail, fine-tune around it, and publish the benchmarks.

  • spin-forbidden reactivity
  • two-state reactivity
  • chirality-induced spin selectivity
  • MACE
  • UMA

Kinetics that can be tested

An experimentalist can measure a turnover frequency, an apparent activation energy, a reaction order or a kinetic isotope effect. None of them can measure a free-energy diagram. Deriving the first set from the second often shows that the tallest barrier is not the step in control. I build micro-kinetic models on computed networks and push the uncertainty in the barriers through to the observable, so you can see how much the comparison with experiment settles.

  • micro-kinetic modelling
  • degree of rate control
  • energetic span
  • kinetic isotope effects

Molecules on surfaces

Put an ordered organic layer on a metal and the two stop behaving like separate things: the adsorbate shifts the work function, the metal shifts the adsorbate. With Elad Gross's group in Jerusalem I have worked on fluorinated N-heterocyclic carbene and carbodicarbene monolayers on gold, computing what the measured surface properties imply about the binding.

The current strand, with the De Feyter group here in Leuven, is scanning tunnelling microscopy. An STM image is not a structure, and getting from one to the other means modelling the adlayer and the contrast it should produce.

  • STM modelling
  • self-assembled monolayers
  • NHC and CDC ligands
  • work-function shifts

Photochemistry and excited states

Excited-state chemistry runs on a different set of rules: the molecule absorbs, lands on an upper surface, and comes back down wherever the surfaces touch. Predicting the product means finding those crossings, not optimising a barrier.

I started on this at Durham with Basile Curchod, mapping where TD-DFT stops being trustworthy for the excited states of atmospherically relevant molecules. Knowing the limits of the method matters more here than almost anywhere else, because the failure is quiet.

  • TD-DFT
  • conical intersections
  • nonadiabatic dynamics
  • atmospheric photochemistry
